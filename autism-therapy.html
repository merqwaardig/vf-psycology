Behavioral therapy for autism

Does your child need extra support with communication, emotional regulation, social situations, routines, or everyday challenges?

Individualised support for your child

Support built around your child — not a fixed program.

Every autistic child is different, with their own strengths, needs, preferences, and way of experiencing the world — and support should reflect that individuality.

Alongside my private practice, I collaborate with an autism center where I provide behavioral therapy to autistic children. I take time to understand how your child communicates, what they enjoy, what situations are challenging, and what skills could make everyday life easier and more comfortable for them.

The goal is not to make a child behave or communicate in a particular way simply because it is considered more typical. The focus is on helping them develop useful skills while respecting their personality, preferences, and individual way of experiencing the world.

Support that respects your child

Autism is not something that needs to be “fixed.”

My role is to support your child in developing skills that make daily life easier, improve communication, increase independence, and help them feel more confident understanding and expressing their needs. Therapy is adapted to the child, rather than expecting the child to adapt to therapy.

How I can help

How I can support your child.

Emotional regulation

Learning to recognise emotions, notice the signs of becoming overwhelmed, ask for a break, and discover calming strategies that actually work for them.

Communication & connection

Expressing needs, choices, emotions, and preferences more clearly — through speech, gestures, visual supports, or whatever combination works for your child.

Routines & transitions

Building predictability and preparation so changes and daily routines feel calmer, clearer, and more manageable.

Social understanding

Everyday social situations, taking turns, communicating boundaries, and navigating friendships — without hiding who they are.

Working with parents

Practical strategies you can use at home, so your child experiences consistency between therapy and everyday life. You know your child best — your insight is part of the process.

Behaviour as communication

Understanding the child behind the behaviour.

A child may become overwhelmed, withdraw, repeat certain behaviours, become upset during transitions, or struggle to follow a routine — for many different reasons. Instead of looking only at the behaviour itself, we can explore what may be happening underneath it. For example, a child may be:

  • Feeling overwhelmed by sensory input
  • Finding it difficult to communicate a need
  • Experiencing anxiety or uncertainty
  • Struggling with an unexpected change
  • Feeling frustrated because a task is too difficult
  • Needing more predictability or preparation
  • Trying to regulate strong emotions

Understanding the reason behind a behaviour helps us respond more effectively and compassionately.

The whole picture

A holistic approach.

A child’s behaviour and emotional well-being are influenced by many things. I take a holistic approach that considers biological, psychological, and social factors rather than looking at a behaviour in isolation — paying attention to areas such as:

  • Emotional well-being
  • Communication
  • Sensory experiences
  • Relationships and social environment
  • Daily routines and expectations
  • Developmental stage
  • Stress and anxiety
  • Family context
  • Strengths, interests, and individual preferences
Getting started

How we begin, step by step.

  1. Getting to know your child

    We start by understanding your child’s strengths, communication style, interests, daily routines, current challenges, and the areas where support may be most helpful.

    Starts with a free 10-minute call

  2. Meaningful goals, together

    Together we identify realistic and meaningful goals and develop an approach that fits your child and your family — shaped around your child, never a one-size-fits-all program.

    Parents and caregivers are involved throughout

  3. An ongoing, supportive process

    Progress can be a major new skill or something small but meaningful — communicating a need more clearly, coping with a change more comfortably, or completing part of a routine with less support. We review as we go and adjust so it keeps fitting your child.

    Reviewed together as we go
Meaningful progress

What progress can look like.

  • Communicating needs more effectively
  • Recognising and managing emotions
  • Coping strategies for overwhelming situations
  • Feeling more comfortable with routines and transitions
  • More independence in everyday activities
  • More confidence in social situations
  • Expressing preferences and boundaries more clearly
  • Skills that support daily life and well-being

Progress is individual and often gradual. The focus is not on comparing your child with other children, but on meaningful progress based on their own needs and abilities.
