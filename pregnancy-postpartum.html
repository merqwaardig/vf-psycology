Pregnancy, Postpartum & Beyond

Supporting you through pregnancy, birth and the transition into motherhood.

Pregnancy and becoming a mother can be one of the most meaningful experiences in life — but it can also bring uncertainty, vulnerability, exhaustion and emotions that you may not have expected. You don’t have to wait until things feel unbearable to ask for support.

At VF Psychology, I offer psychological support to women during pregnancy and throughout the postpartum period, creating a safe and non-judgmental space to talk about the emotional changes that come with becoming a mother.

A personal understanding

Recently, I became a mother myself to a beautiful baby girl. Becoming a mother has given me a new personal perspective on just how profound this transition can be. Alongside the love and joy, there can also be exhaustion, uncertainty, changes in identity, sleepless nights, new responsibilities and a completely different family dynamic.

It has reminded me that becoming a mother isn’t simply about welcoming a baby into the family — it is also about becoming a new version of yourself.

Every mother’s experience is different, and my own experience does not replace my professional training. But it has deepened my empathy and understanding for the emotional complexity of this period of life. You deserve a space where all of those feelings can be spoken about openly.

What we can explore together

You may be experiencing:

  • Anxiety or constant worry during pregnancy
  • Fear about childbirth or becoming a mother
  • Feeling overwhelmed by the changes ahead
  • Changes in your identity and sense of self
  • Guilt about not feeling the way you expected to
  • Pressure to be the “perfect” mother
  • Difficult or unexpected emotions after giving birth
  • Persistent sadness, irritability or feeling emotionally disconnected
  • Difficulty adjusting to life with a newborn
  • Sleep deprivation and emotional exhaustion
  • Feeling like you have “lost yourself”
  • Changes in your relationship with your partner
  • Changes in family roles and dynamics
  • Feeling isolated or alone
  • Difficulties adjusting to your new responsibilities
  • Anxiety about your baby’s wellbeing
  • Birth-related experiences that were difficult or traumatic
  • Challenges around feeding, bonding or parenting expectations
  • Balancing motherhood with work, relationships and your own needs
  • The experience of becoming a mother while living away from your family or support network

You don’t have to be struggling “enough” to seek support

Psychological support isn’t only for when something is seriously wrong. Sometimes you simply need a place to pause, process what is happening, and make sense of everything that has changed.

Pregnancy and the postpartum period involve significant psychological and social adjustments, and support during this time can help women develop coping strategies, manage stress and strengthen their support networks. Therapy can provide a space to understand your emotions, develop practical strategies, strengthen your sense of self, and navigate this new chapter with greater confidence.

A space for you — not just for the baby

When a baby arrives, so much attention naturally shifts towards the baby’s needs. But the mother needs care too.

  • Your thoughts matter.
  • Your emotions matter.
  • Your sleep matters.
  • Your relationships matter.
  • Your identity matters.

This is a space where you can come as you are — without judgement, expectations or the pressure to have everything figured out.

If you’re an international mother

Becoming a mother while living away from your country, family or familiar support system can bring an additional layer of complexity. You may be navigating motherhood without grandparents nearby, adjusting to a different healthcare system, missing your usual community, or trying to create a new support network in an unfamiliar environment. We can explore these experiences together and find ways to help you feel more supported and grounded.

You don’t have to do motherhood perfectly. You don’t have to enjoy every moment. You don’t have to have all the answers. And you don’t have to go through this transition alone.

If you’re pregnant, recently became a mother, or are simply finding this transition more difficult than you expected, I’m here to support you. Let’s make space for you, too.
