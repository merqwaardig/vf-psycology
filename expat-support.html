Expat mental health support

Living in a new country can be exciting, but it can also feel lonely, disorienting, or emotionally exhausting. If you are finding it harder than expected, you are not alone.

Support for the emotional side of living abroad

Adjusting to a new country is more than a practical change.

Moving abroad is a major life transition. Even when it is chosen and wanted, it can bring stress, uncertainty, and a sense of loss. You may feel proud of the life you’re building while also missing home, or enjoy your new surroundings and still feel disconnected. Both can be true at once.

As an expat myself, I understand that adjusting to a new country involves much more than paperwork and logistics — it can affect your identity, relationships, confidence, sense of belonging, and emotional well-being. Therapy gives you space to make sense of what you’re experiencing and feel more grounded.

When it feels different from what you expected

Difficult emotions can be valid even when good things are happening.

The experience of living abroad does not always match what you imagined. You may feel pressure to settle in quickly or to be grateful for the opportunity, even when you’re struggling. Counseling offers space to explore that gap between expectations and reality without judgment, helping you find a way forward that feels more manageable and authentic to you.

How I can help

Where therapy can support you.

Loneliness & homesickness

Missing home is more than missing a place — it can be routines, language, food, friendships, or simply being somewhere you don’t have to explain yourself. We look at staying connected to what matters while building meaningful relationships where you are now.

Culture shock & adjustment

A new culture, language, routine, and social world can feel disorienting all at once. We work on understanding what you’re experiencing and finding a way of living abroad that feels healthier and more your own.

Identity & belonging

Living abroad can make you question who you are — feeling different, caught between cultures, or behaving differently depending on the language or company. We explore what parts of your identity to hold onto and how you want to grow.

Managing stress

Systems, paperwork, housing, work, finances, and unfamiliar routines can build up until small tasks feel demanding. We develop coping strategies, calmer routines, and realistic expectations while adjustment takes its time.

Relationships after relocation

Distance from friends and family, guilt about missing moments, and relationships that change with time — and moving with a partner or family can add new pressures as each of you adapts differently. Therapy helps you explore this and communicate needs.

Building a support network

Feeling at home has no fixed timeline. Belonging can come from friendships, hobbies, work, community, or everyday confidence. We explore what belonging means to you and what might help you build connection and stability.

Taken seriously

A space where you don’t have to explain everything.

One exhausting part of living abroad is feeling you must constantly explain your culture, your background, or why certain things affect you. Therapy is a space where cultural and emotional differences are taken seriously. Your experiences are shaped by where you’re from, where you live now, the people around you, and the different parts of who you are — and all of that is welcome here.

Meaningful change

What therapy can help you with.

Progress often shows up in small, everyday moments: pausing before stress overwhelms you, noticing when old patterns return, asking for support sooner, or feeling more grounded in who you are. These changes may happen gradually, and what feels meaningful will be different for each person.

The goal is not to adapt perfectly, but to find a way of living abroad that feels healthier and more aligned with who you are.
