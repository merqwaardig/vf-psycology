Child & adolescent psychology

Is your child or teenager struggling with anxiety, emotional regulation, confidence, behaviour, friendships, school stress, or changes in mood?

Support that grows with your child

A supportive space to understand what’s happening.

Childhood and adolescence are periods of constant development, and young people do not always have the words to explain what they are feeling. Children may show that something is difficult through behaviour, emotional outbursts, worries, changes in routines, or struggles at school.

Teenagers may become more withdrawn, irritable, self-critical, anxious, or uncertain about themselves and their relationships. Therapy offers a space to understand what your child or teenager may be experiencing while supporting their emotional well-being, confidence, and development.

Every young person is different, so therapy is adapted to their age, personality, development, and individual needs — and helps them develop practical skills to navigate challenges with more confidence.

The aim of therapy

The aim is not to change who your child is.

It is to help them understand themselves, develop practical skills, grow in confidence, and feel more supported. Therapy is a supportive environment — not a place to be criticised, lectured, or told how to feel — where a young person can gradually talk about what’s difficult, at their own pace.

What we can work on

Areas we can work on together.

Anxiety, worries & fears

Anxiety looks different at every age — from separation and physical complaints in younger children to overthinking, perfectionism, and social worries in teenagers. We work on recognising anxious thoughts, understanding triggers, and feeling better equipped when anxiety appears.

Big emotions & regulation

Some children and teens are quickly overwhelmed by anger, sadness, or frustration. We work on naming emotions, noticing early signs, finding calming strategies, and recovering after difficult moments — without feeling they must suppress how they feel.

Self-esteem, identity & confidence

As children grow, how they see themselves matters more. Therapy strengthens self-esteem, self-compassion, awareness of their strengths, a clearer sense of identity and values, and the confidence to set healthy boundaries and make decisions.

Friendships & social challenges

Making and keeping friends, handling conflict, and coping with social pressure, exclusion, or comparison. Therapy helps a young person understand social situations, communicate more confidently, and build healthier relationships.

School, motivation & academic pressure

Overwhelm from expectations, exams, and performance pressure — or lost motivation, procrastination, and avoidance. We support organisation, coping with perfectionism, confidence in learning, and a healthier balance between school and well-being.

Behavioural support

Behaviour often communicates something. Rather than simply trying to stop a behaviour, we explore what contributes to it and develop helpful responses — routines, self-control, problem-solving, and understanding what the young person needs.

Behind the behaviour

Children and teenagers don’t always say what they feel.

Sometimes, children and teenagers express their emotions through behaviour rather than words. Changes in mood, withdrawal, frustration, or difficulties with daily routines may be signs that something deeper is happening.

Rather than focusing only on the behaviour itself, therapy creates space to understand the emotions and needs behind it, helping young people feel heard, supported, and better equipped to navigate their challenges.

How I work

Approaches I may use.

Solution-Focused Therapy (SFT)

SFT shifts attention toward strengths, possibilities, and what is already working. Together we identify small, realistic goals, and small successes build a sense of “I can do this” — supporting motivation, resilience, and a more hopeful perspective.

Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT)

CBT helps a young person understand the link between thoughts, emotions, and behaviour, and respond to unhelpful patterns differently. It can help with anxiety, negative thinking, self-criticism, and stress — always adapted to the child’s age and level of understanding.

Meaningful change

What might change through therapy.

  • Understanding and expressing emotions more clearly.
  • Managing anxiety and difficult feelings with healthier coping skills.
  • Building confidence, self-acceptance, and emotional resilience.
  • Communicating feelings and needs more openly.
  • Strengthening relationships with family and peers.
  • Facing challenges with greater independence and problem-solving skills.

Progress is not always a straight line — good weeks and difficult weeks are both part of the process.
